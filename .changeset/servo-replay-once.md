"brownout": patch
---

A servo or an HC-SR04 triggered by an Arduino now counts each of the program's pin changes once. Both devices time their input from the microcontroller's ordered pin events, and they read that list once per pass, so when an NE555 in the circuit makes one step solve three times (a rolled-back trial plus the two halves it splits into), every step's events reached them three times. The HC-SR04 showed it: a board's first port write leaves a low-then-high pair of first-seen levels on the TRIG pin, one feed of which merely arms the sensor, but the second feed read the pair's low as the matching fall and scheduled an echo 250 µs after boot — a full phantom ECHO pulse nearly 13 ms before the first real measurement. A servo's decode happened to hold its numbers (a re-fed pulse re-measures the same cycle-timestamped width, and the boot pair's fall lands before its rise, which the width check rejects), but its feed tripled all the same. The raw event read now rides the same once-per-step lifecycle as the clocked-IC and display paths: the microcontroller's advance marks the events owed, the first completed pass hands them out, and the split's remaining passes are told nothing happened. Servos and HC-SR04s driven by a Raspberry Pi Pico take this path too (the display fix's merged events stayed Uno and Nano; this read never did), so they get the same guarantee. A board that loses power was already fenced for these devices and is unchanged, as are circuits without a servo or an HC-SR04.
