---
"brownout": patch
---

Switching off a 555 blinker's power no longer freezes the simulation. On a 9 V battery, opening a power switch in either battery lead, or pulling the chip's ground wire, while the timing capacitor held more than about 4 V stalled it: the status showed SETTLING with fail=1/1 and the clock stood still until the power came back. That capacitor and the two timing resistors were all that still fed the 555, and they could not carry its supply current. Switched on, the chip pulled its own supply below its 4 V turn-on threshold and starved itself; switched off, the capacitor lifted the supply back above the threshold, and the solver never settled between the two. `HeadlessRunner` met the same dead end when a 555 ran on a decoupling capacitor after its rail's bridge to the battery was removed, and stopped about 17 ms later as the capacitor drained through the threshold. Now, when a step fails while a chip's power flips like that, the engine checks, with every capacitor holding its present charge, whether the chip could keep its supply switched on and whether the supply comes back with the chip switched off. A chip that starves itself that way counts as off for that step, and the clock keeps running. A chip whose supply is still rising through its turn-on threshold is not starving itself: that step is rejected exactly as before, so the shorter step that follows turns the chip on at the same moment as before. The exception is a step already at the 10 ns minimum step size, where no shorter step is left: there the chip is held off for that one 10 ns step instead of the clock stopping, and turns on as soon as its supply can carry it. Run in 0.5 ms pieces, `HeadlessRunner` used to stop 6 ms into powering up a 74LS04 from a 9 V battery through 100 Ω onto a 100 µF rail; it now runs on, and the chip lights its LEDs about 12 µs after its rail reaches the threshold. A step the check cannot settle is rejected exactly as before too, so a smaller step can still find the answer, and circuits that never hit this case are bit-identical. This covers the same chips as the unconnected-rail fix: the 555, 74xx and CD4000 logic, the 28C16 and 28C256 EEPROMs, the HD44780 and MAX7219 displays and the LM393.
