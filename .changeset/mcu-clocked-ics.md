---
"brownout": patch
---

Chips clocked by an Arduino or a Raspberry Pi Pico now see every pulse the program makes. The simulation hands a microcontroller's pins to the rest of the circuit once per step, as they stand at the end of a step of up to 100 µs, so a pulse that started and ended inside one step never reached a chip's clock input. An Arduino `shiftOut()` into a 74HC595 makes each clock pulse about 4 µs wide, so its eight clock pulses and the latch pulse after them all fit inside one step: the classic shiftOut example kept its LEDs dark and showed its byte only at steps of 2 µs or less. A MicroPython bit-bang on a Pico lost most of its pulses the same way, and a counter or flip-flop clocked with two back-to-back `digitalWrite()` calls never counted. The 74HC595, 74HC165, 74HC74, CD4017, 74LS161 and 74LS173 now replay every edge the microcontroller made on their inputs during the step, in the order the program made them, before they read their pins at the end of the step, so `shiftOut(dataPin, clockPin, MSBFIRST, 0xB2)` and a latch pulse show 0xB2 at any step size. Two pins changed by one instruction (one port write) change together, as on hardware, and a register clocked by that write samples the levels from before it: RCLK rising in the same write as SRCLK stores the 595's byte from before that shift, as tied clocks do, and RCLK raised by a later instruction stores the shifted byte. An input wired to the chip's own output follows it during the replay, so a CD4017 with RESET on Q5 still wraps at five and a 74HC74 with D on /Q toggles on every pulse. Pins driven by anything else, such as a switch, a clock source or another chip's output, are read at the end of the step as before, so a chip clocked or fed by another chip still sees at most one change per step, and circuits without a microcontroller are unchanged. A program that reads a chip's output in the same step it clocks it, such as `shiftIn()` from a 74HC165, still reads the output from the end of the previous step, and a micro:bit still reaches these chips only through its pin levels at each step, because its program runs outside the simulation and sends no edge timing.
