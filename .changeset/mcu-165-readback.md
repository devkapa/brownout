---
"brownout": patch
---

A program that reads a shift register between its own clock pulses now sees the bits it shifted. A `shiftIn()` from a 74HC165 clocks the chip and reads its Q7 pin inside one microcontroller step, but the register only advanced in the digital pass at the end of the step, so every read returned the level the previous step's solve sampled: a sketch that loaded 0xB2 and read it MSB first got 0xFF at 100 µs steps (every read saw the same stale level), and different garbage at other step sizes. While the same single Arduino or Pico both drives a 74HC165's inputs and reads its outputs, the engine now runs a digital shadow of the chip inside the microcontroller's step: each GPIO write the core captures is fed through the same replay walker the committed pass uses (grouped into writes exactly as the committed pass groups them, by cycle), and the chip's output levels are pushed back into the core's input path, where the next `digitalRead()` or `Pin.value()` finds them — on the AVR through the existing port write listeners and the PIN register path, with no new hooks in avr8js, and on the RP2040 through its GPIO input value. The levels the solve stamps are one replay behind the register, so they are also pushed once at the start of each step: a read before any write in a step sees the register as it stands, not as the last solve stamped it. The committed digital pass remains the state of record; the shadow is thrown away with the step. Only the 74HC165 is shadowed for now — other registered clocked parts (the 74HC595, counters, flip-flops) are write-only from a program's point of view and keep their committed behaviour — and no shadow is installed when the chip's outputs do not reach the microcontroller, when two microcontrollers share any of its nets, or when there is no microcontroller at all: those circuits, including an Arduino with no clocked part, hash byte-identically to before. One measured caveat: a shadow applies the walker's own-output feedback (Q7 wired to DS rotates the register), but its effect cannot be observed mid-step at legal step sizes, because a fed-back bit needs eight shifts of the same register to reach Q7 mid-step; a shadow that spans fewer clocks shows the feedback only through the next step's committed register, which replays it intact. Two boundaries, both inherited from the once-per-step replay and measured: a read AFTER a clock's rising edge but before its fall still sees the pre-rise level (the writes of one instruction are applied together, as on hardware they happen at once — a read between a rise and its fall, which real code can do, is one convention off); and a DAISY-CHAINED pair of 165s (Q7 into the far chip's DS) does not track the near chip's output mid-step — the far chip's serial input is sampled at the step's starting solve, so a chained shiftIn reads the near chip's byte one step late. A single chip's parallel loads and reads are exact.
