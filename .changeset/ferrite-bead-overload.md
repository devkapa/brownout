"brownout": patch
---

A ferrite bead can now fail from overload. Its catalog entry carries a power rating (`p_max`) that the engine never read, so a bead carried any current for as long as the circuit ran: 10 A through the old 0.5 ohm default, 50 W against a 0.25 W rating, never failed it. A bead now dissipates its current squared times the resistance it conducts with (`rDc`; 0.5 ohm for a bead that has none, and never below 1 mohm) and is rated like a resistor: above `p_max` the damage grows by (power / `p_max` - 1) per second, it bleeds off at 1 per second inside the rating, and the bead fails open when it reaches 1. A bead dissipating 1.2 times its power rating therefore lasts 5 s. The failure is the existing `resistor_overload` with the resistor's message, so a host that reports a failed resistor reports a failed bead without a change. A failed bead is open in the transient, operating-point and small-signal analyses until `resetFailures()`. A bead inside its rating is unchanged, and a `p_max` of 0 or less, or none, still means no limit. Only the DC resistance is rated: the model has no frequency-dependent impedance, so it has no high-frequency heating.

The catalog's bead data is corrected as well. The default DC resistance was 0.5 ohm and is now 3.5 mohm, the resistance of the 22 AWG lead on the Fair-Rite 2743001111 the part is modelled on, so a bead in series now drops about 1/143 of the voltage it did. Fair-Rite controls this bead for impedance only and gives it no current rating, so the catalog's 2 A is its own assumption, unverified, and `p_max` is now 0.056 W, the power at twice that current ((2 x 2 A)^2 x 3.5 mohm). A bead at 2 A never fails and the default bead fails above 4 A DC: 5 A (1.56 times the power rating) takes 1.8 s and 10 A takes 0.19 s. A bead saved with its own `rDc`, the old 0.5 ohm for one, keeps it and gets the same 0.056 W rating, so it fails above about 0.335 A.
