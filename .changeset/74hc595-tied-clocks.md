"brownout": patch
---

A 74HC595 with SRCLK and RCLK wired together now runs its outputs one clock behind its shift register, as the real chip does. With the clocks tied, one rising edge clocks both registers, and the storage register samples the shift register before that edge's shift reaches it. TI's data sheet (SN74HC595, SCLS041J, section 8.1) says that with both clocks connected together "the shift register always is one clock pulse ahead of the storage register." The model stored the bit the edge had just shifted in, so the outputs ran one clock ahead of a real board: with the model's storage register starting empty, the first clock after load lit QA when SER was high, and now the outputs stay dark until the second clock. The two tied pins are now read as one input, so a slow edge that sits between the input thresholds for a step (an RC-debounced button, say) clocks both registers in the same step instead of shifting first and latching later. A /SRCLR clear seen in the same step as a tied edge still stores zeros, as it does with separate clocks. SRCLK and RCLK on separate nets are unchanged, including when both rise in one step: a latch pulse just after the last shift stores the shifted byte, as it does on hardware.
