---
"brownout": patch
---

A chip whose ground is wired to a breadboard rail that leads nowhere no longer freezes the simulation. Wiring a 555 blinker grounds-first, with pin 1, the timing capacitor and the LED's cathode on a bottom rail not yet joined to the battery, stalled it: the status showed SETTLING with fail=1/1 and the clock stood still until the rail was bridged. The 555's supply current had no way back to the battery except through the 555 itself, so it could never stay powered. Switched on, it pulled the loose rail up to its own supply and starved itself; switched off, the rail fell back to 0 V and powered it again, and the solver never settled between the two. A chip whose supply has no return path outside it now counts as unpowered for as long as it is wired that way: it and anything it drives stay off, the clock keeps running, and the circuit starts the moment the rail is connected. This covers the 555, 74xx and CD4000 logic, the 28C16 and 28C256 EEPROMs, the HD44780 and MAX7219 displays and the LM393, including several of them sharing the loose rail. It does not yet cover a rail that reaches the battery only through a part that cannot carry the chip's supply current, such as a capacitor or an open switch: switching a running 9 V blinker's power off, or pulling its ground wire, can still freeze it, and a separate fix follows. A logic chip wired that way could also read as powered before, its outputs showing levels while no current could flow; it now reads unpowered, like one whose ground pin is not wired at all. Circuits in which every chip's supply is connected are bit-identical.
