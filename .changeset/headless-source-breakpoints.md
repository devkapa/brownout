"brownout": patch
---

`HeadlessRunner` no longer steps over a source's edges and short pulses. It limited its step size by each source's frequency but did not choose where steps fall, and the engine reads a source at the end of each step, so a step that crossed an edge applied the new level for the whole step, and a feature shorter than a step could be missed entirely. A PWL source has no step limit, so a 0.2 ms pulse at 3 ms never showed in a 20 ms run (22 steps, up to 8.2 ms each), and neither did the 21 us pulse of a one-shot `pulse_source` (`per` 0). A 20 us pulse every 1 ms, from a `signal_gen` or a `pulse_gen`, showed only in the first period: 11.2 us HIGH over 20 ms instead of 420 us. The runner now places steps the way de:volt's browser simulator does. It lands a step 10 ns before each source event (every edge and corner of a `clock`, `clock_gen`, `pulse_source`, `pulse_gen` or `signal_gen`, every PWL point, every 0.1 ms noise sample, and the start of a delayed waveform), then crosses the event in a 10 ns step. Pulses down to about 10 ns wide now show (the 20 us pulse reads 419.6 us HIGH), and a new level arrives at most 15 ns early. Landing on an event costs up to two extra steps (a 1 kHz square or clock into a resistor takes 254 steps over 20 ms instead of 214, a noise generator 613 instead of 413), and a circuit that now sees pulses it used to miss takes the steps its response needs: a 1 kohm, 100 nF RC fed the 20 us pulses takes 2043 instead of 489. Fixed-step runs (`adaptive: false`) take the same short steps at each event, so their samples are no longer evenly spaced around one: a 1 kHz clock at 10 us steps takes a 9.99 us and a 10 ns step at each edge. A circuit with none of these sources steps exactly as before, as does a sine, triangle or DC `signal_gen` that starts at t = 0.
