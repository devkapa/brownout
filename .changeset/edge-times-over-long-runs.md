"brownout": patch
---

On the Rust/WASM core, a 555 (or any part switching at a threshold) whose timing capacitor the core steps keeps its edge times over long runs. Each step's error used to pile up into the crossing time, always the same way: on a battery-powered 555 astable in exact rail mode the edges came about 0.4 to 0.6 ms early after one simulated second, and where the timing capacitor is stepped beside a battery's rail in the default mode a crossing could land a hair short of the threshold the 555 read, delaying an edge by a step. A crossing now lands with the step's certified half-step pair, a step near a threshold is held so its error stays within the tolerance of the crossing's time over four simulated seconds, and the step is re-planned against the rail the circuit actually solved. On the fixtures measured, every edge stays within its tolerance over a second at 1 ms and 1/60 s host steps. A 555 whose timing capacitor the core solves in closed form, as in the SAP-1's clock module, already did and reads as before; the battery SAP-1 costs the same. Boards whose stepped timing capacitors cross thresholds take more steps (the identity corpus about 16 % more CPU).
