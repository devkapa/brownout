"brownout": patch
---

A logic output that drives only capacitors no longer reports a false `output_sag` on its edges. The check confirmed a sag one solve after the output switched, and the simulation's step lags a fast RC edge there: a 74LS04 driving 30 pF is back inside its LOW band 8.6 ns after a falling edge, but a 10 ns step still left the pin at 1.6 V against the 0.8 V limit, so every falling edge raised a sag; driving 100 pF, every rising edge did the same whenever the step was 10 ns. Where the output's net is provably a single RC stage, a sag now counts only if an upper bound of how long it really lasts reaches 10 ns. The bound comes from the output resistance, the capacitance and the capacitor's ESR and leakage, and it starts from the farthest point the edge could have begun at: the capacitor's own state when the solve that first sees the sag began, or the farthest rail or far end. That covers capacitors placed from the catalog with their default ESR and leakage, outputs switched on by an enable, and capacitors still charged from before a reload. A net is a single stage when a 74-series, CD4017, CD4511, CD4060 or 28C-memory output, on supplies from ideal voltage sources, drives nothing but other logic inputs and either one capacitor or several without ESR, each returned to ground, a supply or another fixed voltage, and the simulation uses backward Euler, its default. Every other output keeps the old two-step check, since a series resistor, a pull-up, a second driver or a second capacitor with ESR can make an edge last longer than the bound assumes; a sag shorter than 10 ns can still be reported there, as before. Any sag the bound does not rule out is judged by the old check exactly as before. A sag that ends within a single simulation step is still not seen. One case is not covered: a direct caller that changes a source voltage, or a capacitor's ESR or leakage, between steps without `load()` while a sag is already under way, where that change is what stretches the sag past 10 ns.
