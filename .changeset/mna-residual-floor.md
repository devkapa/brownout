---
"brownout": patch
---

A circuit whose supply drops to 0 V no longer stops converging for good as it settles. Switch an LM317 driving an LED to a 0 V supply and the voltages it has left shrink about 2e14-fold per step; some 20 steps later they are below 1e-308, where floating-point numbers lose their relative precision. The check that each linear solve is accurate compared the solve's error with the size of its solution, and at that size a single rounding step read as an error of 5e-6, over the 1e-8 a step may have, so the step was rejected. A rejected step changes nothing, so every later step met the same state and was rejected too: the simulation stalled with the solver reporting non-convergence, for 16 of 46 load resistors between 50 and 500 ohm. That count is for the solver small circuits use; the one larger circuits use sums the error in another order and got all 46 right, but stalls the same way at other values. The check now compares the error with a scale of at least 1e-300, so those steps pass and the voltages settle at exactly 0 V. Any solve with larger values, which is any circuit carrying a measurable voltage or current, is checked exactly as before, and singular or ill-conditioned circuits are reported as before.
