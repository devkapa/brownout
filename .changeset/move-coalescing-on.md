---
"brownout": patch
---

On the Rust/WASM core, a battery-powered board runs faster. A cell's rail used to re-run every reader's actions at each tiny move of the rail (a part of the battery SAP-1 sees about 250,000 of them in 10 simulated seconds); now the rail itself still settles on its exact value at every step, and a move's actions wait while the rail is within 0.1 uV of where they last ran. On the catalog battery SAP-1 with its program running, that takes 14 % fewer instructions per simulated second (20.1 G to 17.3 G), and all 247,438 logic edges, the output register's sequence of values and every failure, latch and trip record come out as before; edges move by under 1e-8 s, readings by under 0.04 of their tolerance (W0), and LED junction temperatures by under 2e-6 C. Over 6,300 random boards in three sweeps, none read more than 8e-4 of W0 from the old build. Only a board whose supply is a cell with no rail capacitor and no stepped part on its rail is affected; bench supplies, boards with a capacitor across the rail and ideal-supply boards read bit for bit as before. It is on by default; `Engine::debug_set_rail_act_eps(-1)` (a negative budget) switches it off for an engine, which is the previous build bit for bit.
